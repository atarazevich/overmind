# Decisions

## 2026-09-17 — Substrate
Jev (TypeSafe System One) as the per-event judge instead of an LLM. Context: the April Overmind ran an LLM watcher and was too slow and expensive to run on every event; Jev returns typed probabilities in ~300 ms at ~$0.04/day. Local alternatives (GLiNER2.5, constrained-decoding on Qwen 1.5B) noted; not calibrated; to be compared later on the labels this project collects.

## 2026-09-17 — Observe first
Phases fixed as observe → label → inject. No action of any kind before the calibration curve exists.

## 2026-09-17 — Hook process shape
`os.fork` + `setsid` with stdio on `/dev/null`, not `subprocess.Popen` of a second interpreter: `import subprocess` costs 4–5 ms and a second interpreter start ~20 ms, against a 50 ms synchronous budget. `urllib.request` (23–25 ms) is imported only in the child. The wiring and the shebang name `/usr/bin/python3` (system 3.9.6, the agent-notch pattern) because the bare `python3` on PATH is a pyenv shim that adds ~110 ms. Context: measured with `-X importtime` and `perf_counter` around the hook process on 2026-09-17.

## 2026-09-17 — Dashboard direction (owner, after board round 1)
Round-1 board (dense monochrome instrument table) rejected by the owner: "very bad… needs to look a hundred times more readable". New direction, fixed by the owner: **Plotly** for every chart (vendored `overmind/static/vendor/plotly.min.js`, served by the stdlib server, so still offline and no CDN at runtime); **Now, Timeline and History visible simultaneously** on one 1440×900 screen, no folds for the three views; **tooltips on everything** that say in plain words what the element is, what the number means and where it came from. The "stdlib only" rule applies to Python; the page may use vendored Plotly. Server-side aggregation (`/summary`) is dropped; the page computes bins, today-totals and the reliability curve from `/events` + `/labels`.

## 2026-09-18 — Hook v2: the log records interruptions, and asks only what was earned (#16)

Schema **v2**. Three changes, one shape: *read what is deterministic, ask only what a careful person could be unsure about.*

**The owner's escape key is now in the log.** Nothing recorded it before, and it is the best label in the system — deterministic, free, given without being asked. On `UserPromptSubmit` the hook tails the transcript named in the payload and writes `interrupted`, `depth` and `tool_calls` as facts; when `interrupted` is true it asks one question, `intervention` (1.0 = Correction, 0.0 = Nudge), about the register of the message that followed. Depth is a fact beside the answer and is kept **out** of the state on purpose: round 1's whole failure was questions that could read the length of what they judged.

**`intervention` is calibrated, not asserted** (`experiments/intervention.py`, results `intervention-2026-09-18_034009`). All 46 interruptions in the corpus, located in the transcripts and joined to the hand classification of `docs/research/own-transcripts.md`: 45 carry a message, **21 Corrections and 24 Nudges**. On the hook's own state, fires on **52% of Corrections against 8% of Nudges — +44 pp**, mean p 0.574 against 0.189, precision 85%, **AUC 0.89**. `depth`, the free fact, scores AUC 0.64 on the same split, so the question earns its request rather than restating something already on the line. Its misses are one-sided and worth knowing: every false negative is a Correction phrased politely (*"ok, it's fine if you need a ton of subagents…"*, 0.29), while the loud ones read 0.83–0.96. The 0.50 firing line is the worst of the six thresholds swept — 0.30 gives 80% accuracy against 73% — but thresholds are phase 2's to set from labels, so the constant stays where it is and the sweep is on the record. Cost for the whole calibration: $0.0009.

**The question set is the two that were earned plus that one.** `jumped` (+38 pp matched) on Stop, `risky` on a Bash call, `intervention` on an interrupted prompt. Dropped from the live hook: `lost_you`, `too_much`, `yap`, `missed_point`, `spinning`, `caving`, `no_receipts`, `claims_done`, `needs_owner`, `drifting`, `sharp_turn`, `needs_parent_decision`. SubagentStop loses both of its questions and the hook now writes nothing for it. **Touching the wrong thing** moved out of the model and into `overmind/rules.py`, where `experiments/conduct_classes.py` imports the same function it was scored with; likewise the transcript reading, now `overmind/transcript.py`. One implementation per thing, in the package, imported by the experiment — not the reverse.

**A measured number only travels with the state it was measured on.** `jumped`'s +38 pp was scored on five fields — `owner_asked`, `reply`, `tool_calls`, `tool_call_count`, `repeated_calls` — and Jev answers from the whole state, so shipping the question with two of them would have been an unmeasured question wearing a measured number. The state builder is now `judge.stop_state`, called by both the hook and round 2's `r2_state`, and the tail carries back the turn's prose and the owner's own message to fill it (3,594 state and view dumps over the real corpus are byte-identical to what the stored run built). `intervention` is the same arrangement through `judge.prompt_state`. The state a question ships on is not a detail of the caller; it is part of the question.

**Bounding the new I/O — in bytes and in lines.** A transcript here runs to 24 MB and the corpus to 310 MB, so the tail seeks to the last **256 KB** and scans backwards, never forward. The window was chosen from the corpus: the owner's previous message is inside it for 96% of his 46 interruptions (median 44 KB back). Bytes alone do not bound the work, though, because they bound the *records* only while the lines are records: the fullest 256 KB window in the corpus holds 244 of them at ~1 ms, while 256 KB of one-token junk is 20,000 lines and 46 ms of a 50 ms budget. So there is a second bound, `MAX_LINES = 2000`, eight times the fullest real window; the bytes cap what is read and the lines cap what is parsed.

**A guard that stops firing is the failure this project is least allowed to have**, so the two ways the tail can come back empty now say so instead of reading as a quiet turn. `tail_error` names a read that threw — missing file, permission, odd shape — and `tail_exhausted` says the scan reached the edge of the window without meeting the owner's previous message, which is what a prompt larger than 256 KB leaves behind: one torn fragment, `interrupted=False`, `depth=0`, indistinguishable from a clean turn. Reaching the *start of the file* is not exhaustion — there was nothing further back — and that distinction is why the flag is worth having rather than firing on every session's first prompt. Past the edge the counts are lower bounds, which under-reports rather than invents.

**What is free rides on the line, including when the network fails.** `wrong_room` already composes *which* rooms the turn wrote in against where it was; that sentence is now `wrong_room_why` on the line, because a *touching the wrong thing* signal that cannot say which thing is no use on a dashboard. The rules' verdicts also ride the error lines beside the facts — a rule that cost nothing is not worth losing to a failed Jev call. `state_source` is gone: it said "payload" or "payload+tail", which `tail_error`'s presence already says, and nothing read it.

Measured, not assumed, old and new interleaved on the same machine: `prepare()` 0.30 ms median, worst 2.14 ms on the 24 MB transcript (it now reads the turn's prose and his message too, which the state needs); the whole parent process, which is what Claude Code waits for, **32.9 ms median either way**, p95 41 ms, against a 50 ms budget — a bare `python3 -c pass` is 22 ms of it.

## 2026-09-18 — The tail reaches his message; Wants you is read again (#17)

Every number below comes from one stored run, `experiments/results/tail-reach-2026-09-18_203657` (`experiments/tail_reach.py`, load average 4.8–5.6 while it ran), or from `wants-you-2026-09-18_202131` (`experiments/wants_you.py`). The run replays 3,672 moments the hook fires at in the owner's top-level transcripts, each at the length the file had then, against the old tail at 54f7f46.

**Why the 256 KB window ran out on 45% of live prompts: two causes, neither of them the window's size alone.** *First, half of all live prompts are not his.* Claude Code delivers a background agent's return, a `<task-notification>`, through UserPromptSubmit too: 17 of the 35 live prompt lines, 978 moments in the corpus. The hook took each for his prompt and scanned for his previous message, which in an orchestrating session sits far back (median 292 KB, p90 1.3 MB), so 52.8% of them ran out. *Second, the window was sized on the wrong population.* His 46 interruptions land mid-turn (median 44 KB back), but across all his prompts the distance is 65 KB at the median, 632 KB at p90 and 4.6 MB at p99, so 18.2% of his real prompts and 40.0% of Stops ran out as well. The distance is bytes, not records: tool results are 65% of the 303 MB of top-level transcripts.

**The hook's budget is work, not time.** Every Overmind entry is wired `"async": true`. Claude Code's hook reference ("Run hooks in the background", code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks) says an async hook's process is started and Claude Code "immediately continues without waiting for it to finish", and that it "doesn't enforce `timeout`" on it. So the 50 ms synchronous budget protected no latency the owner sees, and `"timeout": 10` is no backstop either. The first build of this fix bounded the scan with an 8 ms clock anyway, and its reach then depended on load: its own stored run on a loaded machine (`tail-reach-2026-09-18_192407`, a bare interpreter start 128 ms median) left 10.6% of his prompts and 29.1% of Stops exhausted, and load is highest when he runs the most agents, which is when the hook fires most. The clock also bounded nothing: it was checked between lines, so reading and parsing one long line ran past it. What a hook must protect is the machine, one interpreter per event across every session at once. So the tail is bounded by three counts, each on one cost, and the same transcript gets the same answer at any load: **8 MB read** (`MAX_BYTES`), **5,000 lines looked at** (`MAX_LINES`, the per-line cost that one-token junk runs up), and **3 MB parsed in all** (`MAX_PARSED`). A line that would take the scan past `MAX_PARSED` is not parsed and ends the scan as exhausted, because it could be his message. A line longer than `MAX_PARSED` is never joined: only its first chunk or two are kept. That is enough to know a tool result, whose marker Claude Code writes 221 bytes into the line. CLAUDE.md's hook rule now says this.

**What changed.** A prompt `transcript.is_notification` recognises writes no line and no longer overwrites the prompt cache the next Stop reads as his request (one of the 13 cached prompts on disk is still a subagent's return from before). The predicate matches the whole shape and nothing else: it opens with `<task-notification>`, and after the last closing tag comes nothing, or one of Claude Code's trailer lines ("Full transcript available at: …", "Read the output file to retrieve the result: …"). The first build dropped any prompt starting with a transcript junk prefix, which would have dropped a message he typed beginning "API Error:", left his previous request in the cache, and had the next `jumped` asked about the wrong message. The payload log shows why "nothing" must count too: of 230 notification payloads in `~/.claude/logs/user_prompt_submit.json`, 135 end at the closing tag and 95 carry one trailer line, and all 978 notification records in the transcripts end at the tag. The tail reads backwards 256 KB at a time until his message. A line carrying `"type":"tool_result"` is skipped without a parse: those bytes can only be JSON structure, because inside a string every quote is escaped. A tool result is never his message, and none of the 43,675 tool-result lines in the corpus, subagents' included, is an interruption.

**Measured.** Exhausted, before → after. These rates are exact, because both tails are bounded by counts:
- Prompt lines written: **35.6% → 0.3%** (692 of 1,944 before, notifications included; after, only his 966 prompts write a line, and 3 of them are exhausted).
- His prompts alone: 18.2% → 0.3%.
- Stops: **40.0% → 0.3%** (6 of 1,728).
- Notifications, which no longer write a line: 52.8% → 0.2%.

Every moment still exhausted, 11 of 3,672, is stopped by `MAX_BYTES`: his message is more than 8 MB back. The count bounds were swept one at a time with the others as shipped. `MAX_LINES` 1,000 leaves 0.5% / 1.3% / 1.0% exhausted (prompts / notifications / Stops), and 2,000 already equals the floor the byte cap sets; 5,000 is that with room. `MAX_PARSED` 1 MB leaves 0.9% / 5.1% / 3.1%, and 2 MB already equals the floor; 3 MB is that with room. On the live log replayed, prompt lines go from 17 of 35 to 0 of 18, the 17 task-notifications writing no line, and Stops from 13 of 28 to 0 of 28. The replay reproduces the logged depth on all 17 clean prompt lines.

The whole parent process, old and new interleaved, 30 launches each, median wall / median CPU in ms, on the same loaded machine, where a bare `/usr/bin/python3 -c pass` took 122 / 94:

| Case | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| His message three records back | 181 / 147 | 181 / 146 |
| The same, with a 7 MB tool result between | 173 / 136 | 185 / 150 |
| The largest real transcript (22 MB) | 180 / 147 | 193 / 155 |
| 8 MB of one-token lines | 210 / 163 | 244 / 191 |
| 8 MB of records that all need a parse | 183 / 143 | 258 / 208 |

The typical case costs nothing more. The 7 MB tool result costs 14 ms of CPU and is read past: the hook reaches his message and writes `depth` 2 with no `tail_exhausted`. The two adversarial shapes cost 28 and 65 ms of CPU more at this load, and that is their ceiling, because the bounds are counts. The old tail read 256 KB and so never paid it, and never reached him either. Tail cost per moment in-process: median 4.0 ms, p95 26.8 ms (before: 3.8 and 9.6).

**`jumped` now sees the turn it was scored on.** 14 of the 15 clean live Stop lines replay 1–2 assistant records deeper than they were logged, because a record's timestamp is when it was made and not when it reached the disk: when Stop fires, the turn's last records are often not on disk yet. Claude Code's hook reference says the same of `transcript_path` and names `last_assistant_message` as the way to get the final text. On the 15 clean live Stops, cutting the replay back by the logged shortfall reproduces the logged depth on all 15. The `reply` the hook built then equalled the complete turn's in 1 of 15. So a Stop now closes `reply` with the payload's `last_assistant_message`, unless the last prose on disk is that same message (`transcript.same_message`), and the reply then equals the complete turn's in 15 of 15. That complete turn is the state `jumped`'s +38 pp was measured on. A Stop's `depth` is still short by one or two; it is a fact on the line, not part of the question's state. `<cross-session-message>` and the older `<agent-notification>` also reach UserPromptSubmit (one each in 3,932 logged prompts) and are not notifications to the predicate.

**Wants you.** Rule: `rules.wants_you` on the Stop payload's `last_assistant_message`, written to the Stop line as the fact `wants_you` (absent when an older Claude Code sends no such field). First strip what is not said to him: code blocks, table rows, quoted lines, and a draft for someone else, meaning a reply that ends on a horizontal rule. Then the rule fires when the last paragraph holds a question mark, or an ask such as *want me to, should I, your call, tell me, let me know, say the word, waiting on you, скажи, напиши, кинь, хочешь, жду твои*. Its cost is linear in the reply and small: the horizontal-rule pattern's whitespace no longer crosses lines, which made it quadratic in a run of blank lines, and the ask phrases, whose unanchored Cyrillic half costs about 0.5 µs a character, read only the last 2,000 characters of the last paragraph. The longest last paragraph among the 1,072 distinct messages in `stop.json` is 968 characters, and the held-out score did not move.

`experiments/wants_you.py` scores it against 200 distinct Stops drawn at random from `~/.claude/logs/stop.json`. Each was read in full and labeled before any rule was written, by the agent that then wrote the rule, to one definition: the message leaves open a question to him, or an ask to decide, approve, supply or do something the work waits on. 49% want him. The rule was written on the first 100 and is scored on the other 100:

| | Accuracy | Precision | Recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Held out | **0.89** | 0.87 | 0.92 |
| All 200 | 0.91 | 0.92 | 0.89 |

The survey's rule, *the last line ends in a question mark*, scores 0.73 on the first half and 0.77 held out (0.75 overall), so its "wrong 3 times in 10" replicates. Its errors are 49 misses and one false alarm, recall 0.50 at precision 0.98, because an agent that needs him often asks without a question mark ("Кинь вывод", "Your call — …").

The new rule's errors have names. Six of the seven held-out false alarms are conditional offers ("say the word if you want X"), which the labels count as not waiting on him. Three of the four misses are asks the phrase list does not hold ("Answer those three", "until you say go", "Keep saying `now`"); the fourth is a question followed by a closing paragraph. It ships as a fact because it is right nine times in ten on Stops it never saw, against three in four for the question-mark rule. That 0.89 is optimistic: the labels and the rule have one author, so the number measures agreement with that author's reading, not with the owner's. His marks on the dashboard are what will measure it. Whether a conditional offer should raise the alarm is his call too.

## 2026-09-17 — Conduct classes, round 2: what the matched control killed (#15)
Round 1 compared a corrected turn against a *completed* turn. A corrected turn is truncated by definition, so the comparison measured turn length. Round 2 compares each correction at depth *n* against clean turns cut back to depth *n* — 12 per correction, matched on tool-call count (median difference 0, 96% within one call), 209 in all — and re-scores round 1's wording unchanged under both controls so the artifact is measured rather than argued about. Four full runs, the same 17 verdicts every time, numbers within 6 pp. 448 turn views, 896 requests, $0.072, 908 ms median, 168 s wall.

**Round 1's flagship was an artifact.** `lost_you` +59 pp re-measures as +58 pp naive and **+9 pp matched**, firing on 81% of *matched clean* turns: a turn cut off mid-run has no closing statement of what was done, and neither does any clean turn cut to the same depth. Round 1's negatives were the same artifact upside down — `yap` −66 → −15, `no_receipts` −39 → +10, `too_much` −38 → −10. Between 28 and 52 points of each of those numbers was turn length.

**Kept.** `jumped` — the only class whose lift *grows* under matching: +17 pp naive against +24 pp matched on round 1's wording, +38 pp rewritten, paired win 0.79, fires on both ACTED-NOT-ANSWERED corrections, 0.97 on the "Why the fuck you writing" turn. Its precision inside the corrections is 20% — it fires on half the corrections that were about something else, so it separates corrections from clean turns, not one complaint from another. `wrong_room` **as a deterministic rule** — paths written against paths named: +19 pp matched, 2% base rate, 2 of 3 WRONG-TARGET corrections. The same class as a model question fires on 89% of everything, so the rule replaces it.

**Dropped, one line each on what we learned.**
- `lost_you` — Gone dark was truncation, not opacity. Clean turns cut to the same depth read exactly as dark; the statement of what happened lives at the end of a turn, and he interrupts before it.
- `too_much` — the scope question fires on 47% of his corrections and 38% of depth-matched controls: doing more than asked is the ordinary shape of a turn, not the thing he stops. The complaint is his most frequent one and remains real; we have no detector for it.
- `yap` — reply length carries nothing at the moment of escape: 11% of corrections against 10% of matched controls, unchanged anywhere between 800 and 2500 chars and 2× to 6× the request. Round 1's −68 pp measured the length of the turn, not the length of the reply.
- `missed_point` — leading the state with his own words at twice the budget moved it off zero (0% → 16% of corrections) but onto the wrong turns: 0 of the 3 DONT-GET-IT corrections it exists for. The gap between what he asked and what came back is not legible inside one turn.
- `caving` — built for the public complaint, not for him, and it fires more on matched clean turns (27%) than on his corrections (16%). The corpus said he has never once complained about it; the measurement agrees.

**On probation.** `spinning` — zero fires on 19 corrections, as in round 1: the public evidence keeps it alive, his data holds no instance. `no_receipts` as a rule — 0 of 19, which is a missing label rather than a broken rule: he has never stopped a turn over an unbacked claim, so the flagship honesty class has no ground truth in his corpus and has to be validated against the public complaint instead. Its known error mode is delegation — a commit a subagent made reads as unbacked.

**Two judgment calls, named.** `too_much` was re-tested although #15 listed it neither as deterministic nor as ambiguous; dropping the owner's most frequent complaint on a wording bug would have been the expensive mistake. And each deterministic rule is reported with its own accuracy against the hand tags, not only with a lift — `wrong_room` 2/3 recall at 50% precision within corrections, `yap` 1/3, `no_receipts` unmeasurable — because an unmeasured rule is a guess in a different font.
