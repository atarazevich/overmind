Now

ⓘTurn signals are the raw sense data: on every hook event Jev answers each signal with a probability, and 0.5 or more fires. Session states are what you glance at: Status is a rule in code over a session's recent signals — Wants you when the last reply is Waiting on you and you have not answered since, Off course when Drift fired on two of the last three replies, Spinning when Stuck fired on two replies in a row, Working when the session logged something in the last ten minutes. A session silent for two hours is Quiet and leaves this list. The seven signal columns show each signal's latest probability in the session, in colour when fired; press y if a fired signal was right, n if wrong, s to skip. Every mark feeds Trust in History. Rows sort by state, then by the strongest fired value. Click a row for the numbers of its last reply. Which session wants you. Sorted by state, then by the strongest fired signal.

Timeline

ⓘThe raw sense data over time: one dot per turn signal per event, at the time of the event and the probability Jev returned, 0 to 1. Red dots fired (0.5 or more), grey dots did not; the legend under the chart names the signals and a click hides or shows one. Choose a session with the chips; All sessions shows points only, because a line across sessions would mean nothing. Here a session's Status in Now can be checked against the signals it was derived from. Every judged event of one session as a dot at its probability. Red dots fired.

History

ⓘThe day's metrics: how you learn whether to trust the sense data. Computed by the page from events.jsonl and labels.jsonl each refresh, nothing aggregated on the server: where each signal's probabilities land, how many events ran per hour with Interruptions and Near misses in colour, how fast Jev was (Speed), how right it was (Trust), and what watching cost. Labels close the loop: every y or n you press in Now moves the Trust curve, and Trust decides when a signal will be allowed to act in phase 3. Nothing acts before its curve exists. The day's metrics: where the probabilities land, how much ran, how fast, how right, what it cost.

Probabilities by signal ⓘEach dot is one answer Jev gave today, placed at its probability; red dots fired, the dashed line is the 0.5 firing line. A signal whose points sit near 0 and 1 is decisive; one whose points crowd the middle is unsure and worth labelling first, because its Trust curve will move the most. Hover a point for the event behind it.

Events per hour ⓘHow many hook events were judged in each hour of today, local time. Red is Interruptions: how many times an agent was Waiting on you. Blue is Near misses: how many Risky moves agents attempted. Grey is every other event. Hover a bar for the split. Hours before the first event are empty because nothing was logged then.

Speed ⓘHow fast is the judge: how long each call to Jev took, from sending the state to receiving the answers, measured inside the detached hook process; the session never waits for it. Bins of 100 ms; the dashed lines are today's p50 and p95.

Trust ⓘWhen Jev says 0.8, how often was it right? For each signal, fired calls are grouped by the probability Jev gave and the share you marked right is plotted against it. Points on the dotted diagonal mean the number can be read at face value; above it Jev is too modest, below it too confident. A signal's curve appears once it has 20 labels. Trust decides when a signal may act in phase 3.
